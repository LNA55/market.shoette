Part 1Business summary

My Market Data answers one plain question: “what does this market actually look like, and what place could my product take in it?” — without paying for a consultancy study, and without spending weeks on it. An agent reads the market, identifies its players, estimates their shares, applies the relevant analysis frameworks, then places a project within it and closes with a frank recommendation, one that is able to say no.

The tool serves its author first, testing her own projects before she commits to them — but it is built for any market: everything specific to a subject lives in the data, never in the code. Its value rests on three commitments: dated, frozen reports, which make a market's evolution readable from one cycle to the next; sources cited systematically and estimates flagged as such; and a verdict that decides, where an ordinary market study merely describes.

Part 2Admin documentation

How the tool is built — enough to pick it up again after months away.

Architecture

  • A fully static site. Everything is built locally by the agent, then published by a plain file mirror. No server-side processing, with the single exception of access control.
  • One page per run, self-contained. Each report carries its own styles: it stays readable exactly as published, even when the site's design moves on afterwards.
  • A shared chrome, injected. The header, breadcrumb, four-step bar and footer come from three shared scripts — the site's configuration (brand, markets, pages, labels) sits in a single block at the top of the footer script. Changing the navigation means changing that block, never the forty pages.
  • Navigation pages share one stylesheet (home, market pages, this documentation). Reports, by contrast, are deliberately independent.
  • The positioning chart is a separately versioned engine, reused as-is by every run: it never changes while a report is being produced.

Technical choices, and why

  • A run's data exists twice — inside the page and in a file beside it, strictly identical. The page stands on its own; the file stays usable by a spreadsheet or by the next step.
  • Reports are frozen, but their style is not. An old report can be restyled; its figures are never touched. One exception, tightly framed: step 3 adds a project to an existing report's chart, changing nothing else.
  • Language is read from the address (French at the root, /en/ in English) rather than from local storage: a shared link opens in the right language, and an untranslated page falls back cleanly to its French version.
  • Market pages have marked zones that only the agent rewrites; the rest of the page belongs to its author and is never overwritten.

Known pitfalls

  • The order of the redirect rules matters. Access control catches everything: it must stay the last rule, otherwise old addresses stop being redirected.
  • A redirect rule containing a space must be quoted — without that the server rejects the whole file and the entire site goes down. Any new rule is tested first in a throwaway folder.
  • Publishing never deletes anything remotely. A file removed locally survives online until it is explicitly taken down.
  • Checking the live site requires being signed in: an anonymous request now sees only the sign-in page.

Part 3Product documentation

The idea

My Market Data is powered by a competitive-intelligence agent, designed to be reusable on any market. Its process is split into steps: each does one precise thing, produces a dated deliverable, and feeds the next. The agent works offline — research, analysis, report-building — then publishes the result on this fully static site.

The pipeline, in four steps

  • Step 1 — Read the Market: clear the ground of the market — its reading grids, its main players, the first orders of magnitude.
  • Step 2 — Present the Market: present the market through its key business KPIs and the analysis frameworks relevant to that market.
  • Step 3 — Position MY product in the Market: place my product within this landscape.
  • Step 4 — Strategy recommendation: conclude — strategic recommendations aligned with my goals.

Runs

Each execution of a step produces a run: a frozen, dated report, published on its market's page. Runs are never modified after publication — they snapshot the market at a given moment. Cycle after cycle, they accumulate: a market's evolution is read by comparing its runs.

Data

  • Structured data is kept — each run carries its data canvas (players, dimensions, values), versioned, which feeds the charts and the next steps.
  • Raw data is not kept — articles and pages consulted are discarded after the run; traceability goes through the sources, all referenced and dated in an appendix.
  • Estimates are flagged — the “~” prefix accompanies any estimated value, and a player with no quantified data is listed visibly, never silently omitted.

The site's screens

  • The home page — the list of markets covered, each with its number of runs and the date of the latest one.
  • A market page — that market's runs, arranged by step (1 to 4), oldest first. A step without a run says so explicitly.
  • A step 1 report — assumptions, executive summary and a five-dimension reading grid, a market-share × growth scatter, the interactive positioning chart (you choose what the height, horizontal position, color, size, opacity and border of the markers stand for; clicking a marker opens that player's card), the same data as a table, a reading by segment, then sources and a bilingual glossary.
  • A step 2 report — a band of four key figures, one of them a “shock”, the market definition and its breakdown, the reasoned choice of analysis frameworks, then one card per framework with its diagram.
  • A step 3 report — the positioned project: its value on each dimension and why, a link to the enriched chart, and an analysis of where it lands.
  • A step 4 report — the GO / NO GO verdict up front, the market and positioning summaries, a SWOT, the four Ps of the marketing mix, the recommendations and the partnership or acquisition targets.
  • The language switcher, at the foot of every page: a page not yet translated stays greyed out rather than leading to a dead end.
