Assumptions

  • Reading of the need: “personalizing the products shown to the end consumer” = personalization of the product experience — which products are shown to which visitor: recommendation engines, personalized search, individualized merchandising, commerce-oriented experimentation. Physical product customization configurators (engraving, colors, print-on-demand — Zakeke, Customily…) are an adjacent market and are excluded; so are pure email marketing and off-site ad personalization.
  • Share measurement: share of estimated annual revenue (ARR or turnover, latest known, in US dollars) within a panel of 15 comparable specialist vendors (~1.58 billion $ combined — consistent with Gartner's 1.2 billion $ estimate for “personalization engines” alone in 2024). Most players being private, nearly every value is an estimate flagged “~”, of uneven reliability — noted player by player.
  • The integrated giants sit off-chart: Salesforce (Einstein), Adobe (Target) and SAP (Emarsys) dominate part of the market but publish no isolated figure for their personalization component — they are treated as structuring forces, alongside the “build” alternatives (Amazon Personalize, Google Vertex AI Search).
  • Multi-product vendors: for Optimizely, Insider or Bloomreach, total ARR includes modules beyond strict e-commerce personalization (CMS, marketing automation, CDP). Shares read as the vendor's economic weight in the ecosystem, not as revenue strictly limited to “personalization”.
Section 1

Executive Summary — the reading grid

When an online merchant wants every visitor to see the right products — recommendations, search results, category pages — they buy software from a category Gartner calls “personalization engines”: a market worth 1.2 billion $ in 2024, growing +26%, flanked by a twin market — search & product discovery (Algolia, Constructor, Coveo) — with which it is currently merging: the same battle for “product discovery”, fought from two entry points. Analyst estimates diverge by a factor of 10 depending on scope (from ~2.4 to above 38 billion $ projected for 2030-2033): the category's definition is not settled, and this report anchors itself on the Gartner scope, the narrowest and the most solid.

The market's structure comes in three tiers. At the top, the integrated platforms — Salesforce (Einstein), Adobe (Target), SAP (Emarsys) — sell personalization as a component included in their commerce cloud: a large share of enterprises never buys a third-party tool. In the middle, some fifteen specialist vendors with 20 to 400 M$ of ARR (Optimizely, Bloomreach, Insider, Dynamic Yield, Algolia…) compete for the merchants who want better than the native component — that is this report's panel. At the bottom, an SMB fabric of apps at 18-750 $/month (Rebuy, Doofinder, LimeSpot…) distributed through the Shopify and BigCommerce app stores. The gap in ticket size between tiers is vertiginous: from 600 $/year to ~430,000 $/year — a factor of 700 for the “same” need.

Three recent shifts are redrawing the game. Consolidation is accelerating: three mergers in eighteen months (Klevu+Searchspring → Athos Commerce, Jan. 2025; Monetate+SiteSpect, June 2025; AB Tasty+VWO orchestrated by Everstone, Jan. 2026 — an entity above 100 M$ of ARR). Generative AI is reshuffling the deck: the winners of this cycle are AI-native (Constructor: ARR ×8 in 2024), and “agentic commerce” — buying inside ChatGPT through OpenAI/Stripe's ACP protocol, or through Google/Shopify/Walmart's UCP — threatens to move product discovery off the merchant's own site, which is exactly where these tools operate. Finally, privacy pressure has eased: Google abandoned its plan (April 2025) to remove third-party cookies from Chrome — and on-site personalization, fed by first-party data, was the least exposed anyway.

To compare such heterogeneous players, five dimensions structure the reading:

1Functional breadthPoint solution (search OR recommendations: Algolia, Constructor) → discovery suite (Nosto, Athos) → full experience platform (Insider, Bloomreach, Optimizely). The market's fault line.
2Customer targetSMB (self-service, Shopify apps) → mid-market → enterprise (large merchants, marketplaces). The segment targeted drives price, product and sales channel.
3Entry priceFrom ~0.6 k$/year (Doofinder) to ~150 k$/year (Constructor): a 1-to-250 spread on the entry ticket, which segments buyers radically.
4AI depthFrom merchandising rules to classic ML, all the way to generative and agentic AI in production (Algolia Agent Studio, Bloomreach Loomi, Optimizely Opal). The 2025-2026 battlefield.
5Pricing modelSubscription (traffic/SKU tiers), usage-based (queries — Algolia), or % of revenue generated (Nosto: 2.5-4% of sales). The vendor's incentive structure.

This grid — rather than the bare list of players — is the key to the rest of the report: the following sections position each vendor on these dimensions, then offer a reading by buyer profile — which tool for which kind of merchant, the question at the heart of this run.

    Section 3

    Reading by buyer profile — which tool for which merchant?

    The scatter plot compares vendors; this section answers the run's question from the buyer's side: what an e-commerce company actually buys depending on its size, its platform and its need — bringing back in the integrated giants and the “build” options left off the chart.

    🏢 Enterprise (above 100 M$ of GMV) — the suite, or the component already included

    First reflex: the platform's native component. A merchant on Salesforce Commerce Cloud already has Einstein (recommendations, predictive sorting) included; on Adobe Commerce, Target/Sensei come as add-on modules; the SAP ecosystem pushes Engagement Cloud (formerly Emarsys). This is the first competitor every specialist faces — free in appearance, paid for inside the platform license (revenue share of ~1-3% of GMV at Salesforce). Those who want better buy a dedicated experience platform: Dynamic Yield, Insider One, Optimizely, Monetate — the Leaders of the Gartner MQ Personalization Engines — at 30-430 k$/year (Bloomreach's average ACV ~180 k$/year). Heavy experimentation (server-side A/B) is bought from AB Tasty×VWO, Kameleoon or Optimizely. Documented expected gain: +5-15% of revenue (McKinsey).

    🏬 Mid-market — the product-discovery suites

    The core customer base of the discovery suites: Nosto (recommendations + search + merchandising, billed at 2.5-4% of the sales generated — apparently zero risk, with a real cost that climbs with GMV), Athos Commerce (formerly Klevu/Searchspring, from 699 $/month — SKIMS, West Elm), Algolia on its Grow tier (usage-based search, from ~500-1,000 $/month). Typical budget: 8-50 k$/year. This is the most contested segment — and the one where the search/personalization merger has gone furthest: merchants no longer buy a separate “recommendation engine” and “search”, but one unified product discovery.

    🛍️ SMB / the Shopify ecosystem — the app store as a channel

    Below ~10 M$ of GMV, everything goes through the app stores. Shopify supplies the base layer for free (the native Search & Discovery app: automatic recommendations, filters — capped at 5,000 products, without advanced AI); beyond that, third-party apps take over: Rebuy (15,000+ stores, 99-749 $/month — the Shopify Plus standard), LimeSpot (from 18 $/month), Wiser (9-199 $/month), Doofinder (search from 49 $/month), Clerk.io (119 $/month). Budget: 200 $ to 10 k$/year. Here personalization is a monthly impulse purchase, cancellable at will — structurally high churn, judged on immediate ROI (AOV, conversion).

    🌐 Marketplaces & very large catalogs — the API, or the “build”

    Beyond ~100,000 SKUs (marketplaces, distributors), the need becomes a discovery infrastructure: Constructor (average contract ~150 k$/year — Sephora, Backcountry), Algolia Premium/Elevate, Coveo, Bloomreach Discovery — API-first engines, billed by volume, plugged into composable architectures (MACH). This is also where “build” competes with “buy”: Amazon Personalize and Google Vertex AI Search for Commerce (0.27 $/1,000 queries) offer the raw material on a usage basis, with no license — the option for teams that have ML engineers, and the implicit price ceiling for the whole market. Finally, the 2026 horizon: with the ACP (OpenAI/Stripe) and UCP (Google/Shopify/Walmart) protocols, part of product discovery is migrating to shopping agents off-site — the next battleground for every player on this page.
